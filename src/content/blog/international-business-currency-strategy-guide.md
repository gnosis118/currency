## Types of Currency Exposure

### 1. Transaction Exposure

**Definition**: Risk from outstanding foreign currency transactions

**Examples**:
- Export sales invoiced in foreign currency
- Import purchases in foreign currency
- Foreign currency loans and deposits
- Dividend payments to/from subsidiaries

**Time Horizon**: Immediate to 12 months

**Measurement**:
```
Transaction Exposure = Σ(Foreign Currency Receivables - Foreign Currency Payables)
```

**Management Priority**: High (immediate cash flow impact)

#### Real Example: Export Transaction
**Scenario**: US company sells €1M to German customer
**Invoice Date**: January 1, 2025
**Payment Terms**: 90 days
**Exchange Rate at Invoice**: 1.10 USD/EUR
**Exchange Rate at Payment**: 1.05 USD/EUR

**Impact Calculation**:
- Expected Receipt: €1M × 1.10 = $1,100,000
- Actual Receipt: €1M × 1.05 = $1,050,000
- **Loss**: $50,000 (4.5% of transaction value)

### 2. Translation Exposure

**Definition**: Risk from consolidating foreign subsidiary financial statements

**Sources**:
- Foreign subsidiary assets and liabilities
- Goodwill and intangible assets
- Intercompany loans
- Investment in foreign operations

**Accounting Impact**:
- Balance sheet translation adjustments
- Cumulative translation adjustments (CTA)
- Earnings per share volatility
- Debt covenant compliance

**Management Approach**:
- Net investment hedging
- Balance sheet hedging
- Functional currency optimization

#### Translation Methods Comparison

| Method | Assets | Liabilities | Income Statement | Equity Impact |
|--------|---------|-------------|------------------|---------------|
| Current Rate | Closing Rate | Closing Rate | Average Rate | High |
| Temporal | Mixed Rates | Closing Rate | Historical/Average | Medium |
| Current/Non-Current | Mixed Rates | Mixed Rates | Average Rate | Low |

### 3. Economic Exposure

**Definition**: Risk to firm value from unexpected exchange rate changes

**Components**:
- **Operating Exposure**: Impact on operating cash flows
- **Competitive Exposure**: Market share and pricing power changes
- **Strategic Exposure**: Long-term competitive position

**Time Horizon**: 1-5+ years

**Complexity**: Highest (requires scenario analysis and modeling)

#### Economic Exposure Analysis Framework

**Step 1: Identify Value Drivers**
- Revenue sources by geography
- Cost structure by currency
- Competitive dynamics
- Market positioning

**Step 2: Scenario Modeling**
- Base case: Current exchange rates
- Stress scenarios: ±10%, ±20% currency moves
- Extreme scenarios: ±30%+ moves
- Correlation analysis across currencies

**Step 3: Sensitivity Analysis**
```
Economic Exposure = ∂(Firm Value) / ∂(Exchange Rate)
```

**Step 4: Strategic Response Planning**
- Operational adjustments
- Market repositioning
- Supply chain optimization
- Pricing strategy changes

### Net Exposure Calculation

**Formula**:
```
Net Exposure = (Foreign Currency Inflows) - (Foreign Currency Outflows) - (Existing Hedges)
```

**Example: EUR Exposure Analysis**
- EUR Revenue: €25M annually
- EUR Costs: €8M annually
- Existing EUR Hedges: €12M forward contracts
- **Net EUR Exposure**: €25M - €8M - €12M = €5M

## Hedging Strategies and Instruments

**Characteristics**:

- No upfront premium
- Obligation to execute
- OTC instrument

**Best Use Cases**:
- Known future cash flows
- Budget rate protection
- Accounting hedge designation

**Pricing Formula**:
```
Forward Rate = Spot Rate × (1 + r_domestic × t) / (1 + r_foreign × t)
```

#### Forward Contract Strategy Example

**Market Conditions**:
- Spot EUR/USD: 1.0800

- Buy €10M put option, strike 1.0700

- **Fixed-for-Floating**: One fixed, one floating rate
- **Floating-for-Floating**: Both legs floating rates

**Applications**:
- Long-term hedging (2-10 years)
- Funding cost optimization
- Balance sheet management
- Regulatory capital efficiency

### Advanced Hedging Strategies

#### 1. Dynamic Hedging

**Concept**: Adjust hedge ratios based on market conditions

**Implementation**:
- Delta hedging for options portfolios
- Volatility-based adjustments
- Correlation-driven rebalancing
- Automated execution systems

**Example Algorithm**:
```python
def dynamic_hedge_ratio(volatility, correlation, time_to_expiry):
    base_ratio = 0.5  # 50% base hedge
    vol_adjustment = (volatility - 0.15) * 2  # Adjust for vol
    corr_adjustment = correlation * 0.3  # Adjust for correlation
    time_adjustment = (1 - time_to_expiry) * 0.2  # Increase as expiry approaches
    
    hedge_ratio = base_ratio + vol_adjustment + corr_adjustment + time_adjustment
    return max(0, min(1, hedge_ratio))  # Bound between 0 and 1
```

#### 2. Portfolio Hedging

**Approach**: Hedge net exposure across all currencies

**Benefits**:
- Reduced hedging costs
- Natural offsets recognition
- Simplified management
- Better capital efficiency

**Implementation Steps**:
1. Calculate net exposure by currency
2. Identify natural hedges
3. Hedge residual net positions
4. Monitor and rebalance regularly

#### 3. Proxy Hedging

**Use Case**: When direct hedging is unavailable or expensive

**Examples**:
- Hedge CNY exposure with HKD
- Hedge emerging market basket with index
- Use major currency as proxy for minor

**Correlation Requirements**:
- Minimum 0.7 correlation
- Stable relationship over time
- Similar economic drivers
- Regular effectiveness testing

#### Cash Flow at Risk (CFaR) Model

**Formula**:
```
CFaR = √(Σ(Exposure_i² × Volatility_i² × Correlation_ij))
```

**Where**:
- Exposure_i = Net exposure in currency i
- Volatility_i = Historical volatility of currency i
- Correlation_ij = Correlation between currencies i and j

**Example Calculation**:
- EUR Exposure: €10M, Volatility: 12%
- GBP Exposure: £5M, Volatility: 15%
- EUR/GBP Correlation: 0.8
- USD Equivalent: EUR €10M = $11M, GBP £5M = $6.25M

```
CFaR = √((11M × 0.12)² + (6.25M × 0.15)² + 2 × 11M × 6.25M × 0.12 × 0.15 × 0.8)
CFaR = √(1.76M² + 0.94M² + 2.48M²)
CFaR = √6.18M² = $2.49M
```
