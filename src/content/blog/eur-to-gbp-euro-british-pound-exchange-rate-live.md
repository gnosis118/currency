## Understanding EUR/GBP After Brexit

EUR/GBP (inverse of GBP/EUR) tracks euro strength against the pound. Post-Brexit, this rate reflects UK's reduced EU integration and evolving trade relationship. Currently trading 0.85-0.87, representing modest euro strength versus pre-2016 levels.

### Why EUR/GBP Matters

**EU Tourism to UK**: 9 million EU visitors spend £12 billion annually. Stronger euro (higher EUR/GBP) makes UK more affordable for European tourists.

**UK Property Investment**: EU buyers account for 15% of London property purchases. Exchange rates significantly impact investment returns and affordability.

**Cross-Channel Trade**: €620 billion UK-EU trade creates massive currency exposure for businesses on both sides.

**Expat Finances**: 3.6 million EU citizens in UK manage euro income, pensions, or savings. Exchange rates affect their UK purchasing power.

**Student Mobility**: 150,000 EU students study in UK (post-Brexit decline from 200,000+). Tuition fees, living costs all affected by EUR/GBP.

## Current Market Drivers

### ECB vs Bank of England Policy

**Rate Differential**: ECB 4.0% vs BoE 4.75% creates 75bp gap favoring pound. Higher UK rates attract capital, supporting pound (lowering EUR/GBP).

**Inflation Paths**: Eurozone inflation 2.4% vs UK 3.9% (services-driven). Higher UK inflation without proportionally higher rates can weaken pound purchasing power.

**Balance Sheet Policy**: Both reducing QE, but ECB's APP larger (€5 trillion vs BoE £800 billion). Relative tightening affects currency.

### Economic Growth Divergence

**GDP Growth (2024-25)**:
- Eurozone: +0.8% (German weakness offset by Southern resilience)
- UK: +0.5% (Brexit adjustment drag continues)

Weaker UK growth typically pressures pound, but market has largely priced in post-Brexit "new normal."

**Sectoral Performance**:
- **Manufacturing**: Germany struggles, UK stable but smaller base
- **Services**: UK financial services resilient despite Brexit
- **Construction**: Both facing headwinds from higher rates

### Political Factors

**UK Stability**: After 2016-2024 volatility (5 PMs, 3 snap elections), relative calm supports pound.

**EU Cohesion**: French-German alignment, Southern Europe fiscal concerns, Eastern expansion all influence euro strength.

**Trade Negotiations**: UK-EU relationship evolution affects sentiment. Smoother cooperation supports both currencies; friction creates volatility.
