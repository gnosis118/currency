## What is Currency Conversion?

Currency conversion is the process of exchanging one currency for another at a specific exchange rate. This rate determines how much of one currency you'll receive in exchange for another.

### Key Components:
- **Base Currency**: The currency you're converting from
- **Quote Currency**: The currency you're converting to
- **Exchange Rate**: The price of one currency in terms of another
- **Spread**: The difference between buy and sell rates

## How Exchange Rates Work

Exchange rates fluctuate constantly based on various economic factors:

### Primary Factors Affecting Exchange Rates:

1. **Economic Indicators**
   - GDP growth rates
   - Inflation levels
   - Employment data
   - Trade balances

2. **Political Stability**
   - Government policies
   - Political events
   - International relations

3. **Market Sentiment**
   - Investor confidence
   - Risk appetite
   - Speculation

4. **Central Bank Actions**
   - Interest rate decisions
   - Monetary policy
   - Currency interventions

### Real-Time Rate Example:
As of today, 1 USD = 0.85 EUR, meaning you need $1.18 to buy €1.

## Types of Exchange Rates

Understanding different rate types helps you make informed decisions:

### 1. Spot Rate
- Current market rate
- For immediate transactions
- Most volatile

### 2. Forward Rate
- Agreed rate for future transactions
- Protects against rate fluctuations
- Used for hedging

### 3. Mid-Market Rate
- Average of buy and sell rates
- Most accurate "true" rate
- Used by financial institutions

### 4. Tourist Rate
- Rates offered to retail customers
- Includes provider margins
- Usually less favorable

## Hidden Fees to Avoid

Currency conversion often involves hidden costs that can significantly impact your exchange:

### Common Hidden Fees:

1. **Exchange Rate Markup**
   - Providers add 2-4% to mid-market rate
   - Biggest hidden cost
   - Compare rates carefully

2. **Transfer Fees**
   - Fixed fees per transaction
   - Can be $10-50 per transfer
   - Some providers waive for large amounts

3. **Receiving Fees**
   - Charged by recipient's bank
   - Often $10-25
   - Not always disclosed upfront

4. **Intermediary Bank Fees**
   - Charged by correspondent banks
   - $15-30 per transaction
   - Common in international transfers
