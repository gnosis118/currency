For SME treasury management, forward exchange contracts can lock guaranteed conversion rates for future international payments or receipts. This removes the risk that currency swings wipe out profit margins or create cash‑flow shortfalls.

## How much do forward contracts typically cost SMEs, and what drives pricing differences between banks?

Standard forwards have no upfront premium (unlike options). Typical costs:
- Setup/administration: $0–$500 depending on relationship and transaction size
- Bid–ask spread embedded in the forward quote: roughly 0.10%–0.80% for major pairs

Why pricing differs across banks:
- Credit assessment of your company (better credit = tighter spreads)
- Relationship depth and overall wallet share (bigger relationship = preferential pricing)
- Transaction size (larger tickets = lower spreads)
- The bank’s FX inventory and balance‑sheet needs at the time of quoting

Regional banks can be very competitive for established commercial customers. Money‑center banks often price best for larger tickets (>$100k). Some credit unions offer excellent pricing for members, but may have limited currency availability or slower processing.

## Can I cancel or modify a forward contract if my business circumstances change?

Standard forwards are binding legal commitments—you cannot unilaterally cancel them. Options if things change:
- Close the forward with an offsetting trade; you’ll realize a gain or loss vs. the contracted rate
- Request amendments (date/amount) with bank approval; expect fees and rate adjustments
- Complete the original forward and manage surplus/shortfall with spot trades

This is the tradeoff: complete rate certainty in exchange for zero flexibility. If transaction timing/amounts are uncertain, consider currency options instead (flexible, but you pay an upfront premium).

Some banks offer “window forwards” or “flexible forwards” that allow delivery within a date range or in partial draws—useful middle ground.

## Should I hedge 100% of my FX exposure, or is partial hedging more appropriate?

Most SME treasury teams hedge 50–80% of predictable exposure—balancing risk reduction with upside participation.

Rules of thumb:
- Thin margins (<8%): hedge 75–90%
- Higher margins: hedge 40–60%
- Adjust dynamically: raise hedge ratios during volatile periods; trim during quieter markets

## How far ahead can I lock forward rates, and what are the practical limits?

Large banks typically offer 2–5 year tenors for creditworthy SMEs. Liquidity and pricing are best inside 12 months. Common practice:
- Importers/exporters: 90–365 days to match order/cash cycles
- Service businesses with quarterly invoicing: 30–180 days

Longer tenors mean wider spreads, higher credit requirements, more basis risk if the underlying business changes, and potential hedge‑accounting complexity. Very short tenors (<30 days) may not justify setup/documentation efforts; very long (>2 years) often require substantial credit support and may price worse than rolling shorter hedges.

## What if my business doesn’t need the hedged currency at maturity?

You’re still obligated to exchange per the contract. This creates basis risk—the hedge may no longer match your commercial exposure.

Options:
- Take delivery, then do a spot trade to convert back (incurs extra costs)
- Reallocate the currency inside your business (e.g., pay other suppliers)
- Use it for other purposes or unwind/modify the forward with the bank (realizing P/L)

Accurate FX forecasting is essential. Firms with uncertain timing often prefer options because they can be abandoned; forwards cannot.

## How do I evaluate whether my bank is offering competitive forward rates?

- Compare quotes against mid‑market forward rates; for major pairs and amounts ≥$50k, look for 0.10%–0.50% all‑in spread
- Get 2–3 competing quotes for tickets ≥$50k; benchmark total costs including any fees/collateral
- Evaluate execution/service: speed, settlement accuracy, platform quality
- Consider your total relationship value—deposits, lending, and fee business often translate to better FX pricing

## Bottom line

Forward contracts are essential risk management tools for SMEs with margins under ~15%. If quarterly currency swings can exceed your margin, hedging moves from optional to mandatory.

A systematic approach beats ad‑hoc decisions:
- Define hedge thresholds and authorization limits
- Maintain 2–3 bank relationships for pricing leverage
- Focus on transactions ≥$25k where costs justify the certainty benefit
- Use simple forwards for known amounts/timing; add options when timing is uncertain and a 1–3% premium is acceptable for flexibility

Treasury policy should match complexity and exposure size. Firms with $1–10M annual international activity need basic policies; larger firms need full frameworks (effectiveness testing, performance metrics, crisis playbooks).

Risk‑management mindset: hedge to protect core profitability, not to speculate. Disciplined programs typically reduce quarterly earnings volatility by 30–50% and improve cash‑flow predictability.
